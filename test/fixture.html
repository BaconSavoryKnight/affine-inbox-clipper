How Sourdough Works

Sourdough is bread leavened by a culture of wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria rather than commercial baker's yeast. The process is slow, and the slowness is the point.

A starter is a stable community of microbes living in flour and water. Feed it regularly and it will raise bread for decades; neglect it and the acidity climbs until the yeast give up.

The microbes

Yeast produce carbon dioxide that inflates the dough. Lactobacilli produce lactic and acetic acid, which give the bread its tang and help it keep longer than yeasted loaves do.

Yeast species

Most starters are dominated by Kazachstania humilis or Saccharomyces cerevisiae, depending on the flour and the baker's hands and the local environment.

Feeding schedule

  • Discard half
  • Add equal weights of flour and water
    • Use rye for a boost
ratio = flour / water

Time is the most important ingredient.

Troubleshooting

If your starter smells like nail polish remover, it is hungry. Feed it more often and keep it a little warmer than you have been doing lately for best results.
